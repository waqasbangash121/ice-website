Decision and scope
20 reusable page templates
 support the national website. These are content and behaviour contracts, not 20 unrelated visual designs. City and suburb service pages use the same local-service template. Pricing uses one template with national and service variants. Blog and guide articles share presentation components but retain different editorial roles.
This is an architecture and template-specification file with separately opening visual demonstrations. Demo copy is not final production copy. It replaces the planning logic of the previous index; it does not change the live site or rewrite existing HTML drafts. Template IDs below are a new registry, A01–A20, to avoid silently changing the old T01–T14 meanings.

Brand application and decision log
This document applies Brand Guidelines v1.1 (16 November 2026). It specifies the website rather than previewing customer pages. The existing logo and licensed fonts are embedded from the supplied website package; no new logo was created.
Standard
Application in this revision
Visual identity
Deep navy #092F4D; lime #94D60C; supporting blue #114D7B; white; pale blue #F5F9FC; pale green #F0F8DF. Navy text on lime actions; no white text on lime.
Typography
Manrope headings; DM Sans body/interface; 16–18 px body target. Website display headings/primary CTA labels use title case; document headings, body text and FAQ questions use sentence case.
Voice
Professional, approachable and calm. Explain what is covered, what may cost extra and what happens next. No unsupported best/cheapest/number-one claims.
Identity
Instant Cleaning Experts in full. Preserve supplied logo proportions and descriptor; use full-colour mark on a white panel, not a fabricated reverse version.
Conversion
Quote form is the primary commercial action; call 1800 934 009 is secondary. Contextual service labels must accurately describe the next action.
Service-specific copy
Preserve approved end-of-lease wording where applicable: “What If Something Included In Your Clean Gets Missed?” and “Get My End-of-Lease Quote”. Do not apply end-of-lease promises to every service.
Estimate language
Indicative estimate → enquiry → team confirms scope, price and availability → booking. “Get My Confirmed Quote” is the approved post-estimate action, not confirmation that a quote is already final.
Claims and proof
Insurance, ratings, statistics, local jobs, guarantees and credentials need current support. Keep proof outside the form; no generated before/after evidence.
Component style
48 px preferred interactive height; 8 px controls, 16 px cards; 1280 px content maximum; readable wrapping and visible focus; restrained borders/shadows.
Accessibility
Target text/control contrast from the guidelines; labels, keyboard support, error recovery, safe-area spacing and equal material information across mobile/desktop. Verify actual implementations later.
Latest instructions take precedence
Conflict / unresolved detail
Decision
Older Central Coast exclusion vs later national coverage
Use the user-confirmed Australia-wide coverage. Do not copy the older Central Coast exclusion into the new site. Coverage is not a promise of an available appointment.
Old mobile call-first bar vs quote-first national website
Use the quote form as primary and call as secondary. The mobile menu gets its own expandable layout; sticky controls must not cover fields or errors.
Population >2,000 scenario
Retain as a planning scenario, not a service-coverage exclusion or an irreversible publication cap. Smaller localities can still enquire.
Existing end-of-lease form vs new universal form
Protect the existing live form. This specification proposes the new shared journey; the user will provide final service-specific questions and integrations.
Existing calculator excl. GST presentation
Record the existing guideline, but require pricing-owner review of the all-service tax/total display before implementation. Keep tax labels and any approved total prominent; do not silently invent a consumer price schedule.
Eligible 72-hour rectification process
Use only for the applicable approved service and current terms. Preserve eligibility and agreed scope; never generalise it to every service or promise full bond recovery.
Missing operational/proof details
Store editorial placeholders in the CMS/admin specification, not in published customer copy. Hide unsupported optional proof or hold affected claims until evidence is approved.

20 templates total.
 Reusable components will include navigation, mobile navigation, service cards, locality finder, pricing tables, scope lists, proof cards, FAQs, breadcrumbs, quote entry and estimator. An estimator is an embedded component, not an extra SEO page template. Confirmation is a quote state, not a new template. XML sitemaps are generated feeds, not page templates. Variants will share the same component library.
Reusable block registry — 44 blocks
4 shared shell blocks + 40 content/interaction blocks = 44 reusable block types.
 A block is a reusable component contract with page-specific data; it is not a paragraph to copy everywhere. These blocks compose the 20 templates. Variants change the data and appropriate presentation without adding a new template ID.
R — required:
 the page needs this answer/function.
C — conditional:
 include only when the specified evidence, variant or feature applies.
O — optional:
 useful enhancement, not necessary for the page’s task. Required answers can be concise; required does not mean a large standalone section.

Shared layout, content and technical contract
All templates inherit G01 header and G02 footer. G04 breadcrumbs apply below the homepage where a meaningful parent exists. G03 quote-first mobile actions apply to commercial pages; they are reduced or omitted on focused quote, support, policy and error states. The page sequences below describe the main content only.
Area
Required behaviour
Mobile menu
Separate expandable Services / Locations / Resources groups; visible menu button and accessible expanded state; Escape closes, focus returns to trigger. Preserve full logo before squeezing labels.
Mobile content
Keep essentials visible: answer, scope limits, price conditions and form errors. FAQs may expand; core qualifications must not rely on opening a hidden answer. Galleries use manual controls, not auto-rotation.
Reading order
Hero → immediate answer/choice → decision detail → supported proof → next action. Adjust to intent; quote and support tasks start with action rather than a long promotional introduction.
CTA rules
Use one primary conversion per task. Commercial pages: universal quote with contextual service/locality. Utility print, contents and filters remain visibly secondary. Support reports and partner enquiries retain accurate task labels.
Form label conventions
Use visible labels and attached required markers. Retain Contact Number; Studio where bedrooms apply; DD/MM/YYYY with aligned calendar. Optional Other reveals a field only when selected, with the existing 500-character limit where applicable. Final universal fields still await the user.
Evidence fallback
Omit unsupported proof blocks, show accurate assessment requirements for unknown prices and require an editorial reason before publishing a new location page. Missing evidence never justifies a fabricated review or local office.
Heading and metadata
One descriptive H1, logical H2/H3 hierarchy, unique intent-led title and description, contextual image alt text. No keyword density target, forced word count or all-city title.
Schema and entities
One central provider record; page-type structured data only when accurate and visible. Coverage is distinct from branch address. Markup is not a ranking guarantee and does not manufacture evidence.
Internal discovery
Published canonical pages have crawlable contextual links and breadcrumbs. Large directories paginate. Maps/search supplement navigation. Filter/query combinations do not automatically become indexable pages.
Private form data
No personal data in URLs, markup or analytics events; use opaque draft references, service-side validation and documented retention. Avoid auto-applying private draft data to another person’s session.
CMS gates
Draft → evidence/intent review → business approval → technical checks → publish. Keep owner, source, approval date, change history and canonical intent on each page record.
Measurement
Track quote entry/completion, estimate handoff and call action without personal data. Later inspect indexation, query overlap and real conversions; template completeness is not a live SEO diagnosis.
Detailed sections for all 20 templates
Each card is a detailed specification for the future customer page, with separate visual examples available through the preview buttons. Sequence numbers set the proposed default reading order. Related short blocks can be combined visually when that improves the flow.

Case studies and reviews: backend structure
Case studies
Select a primary service first. The editor then loads the relevant prompts: for example, end-of-lease inspection scope, carpet material/stain limitations or pressure-washing surface/access. The final page uses A14 with conditional service fields, not twelve different case-study templates.
Record group
Required structure
Identity
Case ID, slug, title, primary service ID, optional secondary service IDs, locality ID, region relationships and job date.
Customer context
Property type, customer objective, starting condition, access constraints and approved scope. Do not publish street address or identifying client information without permission.
Work and evidence
Process actually used, exclusions, before/after assets with captions, dates and publication consent; comparable images rather than manipulated results.
Outcome
Observed result, limitations, customer confirmation where available and linked review record. No invented bond recovery, stain removal or turnaround claims.
Publishing
Author/reviewer, evidence references, customer/media consent, last reviewed date and draft/review/approved/published status.
Discovery
Filter by service, state/region, property type and relevant challenge. Link each case to its service and locality pages; no automatic indexed filter combinations.
Reviews
Record group
Required structure
Identity and provenance
Review ID, source/platform, original source URL, source review ID, date, permitted reviewer display name and exact approved text.
Rating
Value and scale only if the source supplies them; keep unrated testimonials distinct.
Relationships
Service IDs and locality ID only when supported by the review or associated consented job; optional case-study/job relationship.
Trust and governance
Permission/use basis, moderation status, deduplication key, verification method, edited/translated flag and retrieval date.
Display
Filter by service and location; show source/date and clear empty states. Use relevant excerpts on landing pages without changing meaning.
SEO
One main reviews page by default. No automatic page per review or filter. Self-serving LocalBusiness reviews do not qualify for Google review stars; do not promise rich results.
One quote journey across all services
Direct entry:
 /get-a-free-quote/ starts an empty draft and lets the visitor choose the service.
Service/location entry:
 the page passes service ID, locality ID where selected and source route into the same quote draft.
Estimate entry:
 an estimator saves selected inputs, estimate range, assumptions and pricing version, then opens the same quote draft.
Review and edit:
 prefilled details remain visible and editable. Changing scope, location or service invalidates the previous estimate and triggers recalculation.
Submit:
 the server validates the final payload, prevents duplicate submissions and sends it to the agreed lead system.
Confirmation:
 show the received request summary and approved next-step expectations. A quote request is not a booking confirmation.
Use an opaque draft/session identifier and server-side draft storage where feasible. Do not put names, addresses, email or phone numbers into URLs. Same-session back/forward navigation and refresh should preserve the draft with a documented expiry. Retain attribution separately from editable customer selections. Server-side pricing validation is authoritative; do not trust a client-supplied total.
The final questions, estimator inputs, service combinations, consent wording, CRM destination, draft retention and response commitments await your details. This plan sets the journey without pretending those fields have been approved. Include accessible errors, mobile input types, progress/resume in the same session and a secondary call option. Every commercial page uses the form as the primary CTA and calling as secondary; avoid obstructive sticky bars or competing primary actions.
